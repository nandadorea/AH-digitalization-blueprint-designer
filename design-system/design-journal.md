## Design Journal Entry 001

Topic: Blueprint scope and abstraction

Status: Agreed – pending validation through worked examples.

Key observations

Blueprints should be organised around business capabilities, not business processes.
Business processes remain country-specific implementation choices.
A single Blueprint may support multiple operational contexts through Profiles.
Profiles will be illustrated using recurring Sentara context banners.
Separate Blueprints should only be created when the underlying capability changes, not simply because the reporting source changes.

##Design Journal Entry 002

Topic: Outcome-oriented design

Status: Agreed.

Key observations

Blueprints describe the capability achieved after implementation.
They deliberately avoid prescribing surveillance terminology.
Countries should recognise their own systems regardless of the names they use.

What capability should the Veterinary Service have?
Examples:
•	Digitally capture disease suspicion notifications. 
•	Digitally manage outbreak investigations. 
•	Digitally manage laboratory submissions. 
•	Digitally plan active surveillance. 
The Blueprint is centred on the capability.

There will not be a blueprint for each initiator of a disease suspicion (official, community-based, etc),
but we will use PROFILES to exemplify how that capability can be used by differenr workflows.

The Sentara material isn't demonstrating a business process.
It's demonstrating how the same capability can be realised differently.
For example:
*Sentara Profile – Official Veterinary Service*
Notification initiated by a government veterinarian. 
Creates an official investigation immediately. 
*Sentara Profile – Community Animal Health Worker*
•	Notification initiated by a community worker. 
•	Requires veterinary validation before becoming an official record. 
The capability is identical.
The process differs.

Digitalization Blueprints describe capabilities, not organizational workflows.
Everything else follows naturally:
•	Countries adapt the workflows. 
•	Countries adapt the governance. 
•	Countries adapt the terminology. 
•	Countries adapt the software.
What I think we've accomplished today
Without writing a single page of the example Blueprint, we've clarified several foundational ideas:
1.	Blueprints are centred on business capabilities, not business processes. 
2.	Blueprints are outcome-oriented rather than terminology-oriented. 
3.	Profiles illustrate different operational contexts without changing the underlying Blueprint. 
4.	The Running Example (Sentara) is the vehicle for demonstrating those Profiles throughout the Blueprint.
