## Design Journal Entry 001

Topic: Blueprint scope and abstraction

Status: Agreed – pending validation through worked examples.

Key observations

Blueprints should be organised around business capabilities, not business processes.
Business processes remain country-specific implementation choices.
A single Blueprint may support multiple operational contexts through Profiles.
Profiles will be illustrated using recurring Sentara context banners.
Separate Blueprints should only be created when the underlying capability changes, not simply because the reporting source changes.

##Design Journal Entry 002

Topic: Outcome-oriented design

Status: Agreed.

Key observations

Blueprints describe the capability achieved after implementation.
They deliberately avoid prescribing surveillance terminology.
Countries should recognise their own systems regardless of the names they use.

What capability should the Veterinary Service have?
Examples:
•	Digitally capture disease suspicion notifications. 
•	Digitally manage outbreak investigations. 
•	Digitally manage laboratory submissions. 
•	Digitally plan active surveillance. 
The Blueprint is centred on the capability.

There will not be a blueprint for each initiator of a disease suspicion (official, community-based, etc),
but we will use PROFILES to exemplify how that capability can be used by differenr workflows.

The Sentara material isn't demonstrating a business process.
It's demonstrating how the same capability can be realised differently.
For example:
*Sentara Profile – Official Veterinary Service*
Notification initiated by a government veterinarian. 
Creates an official investigation immediately. 
*Sentara Profile – Community Animal Health Worker*
•	Notification initiated by a community worker. 
•	Requires veterinary validation before becoming an official record. 
The capability is identical.
The process differs.

Digitalization Blueprints describe capabilities, not organizational workflows.
Everything else follows naturally:
•	Countries adapt the workflows. 
•	Countries adapt the governance. 
•	Countries adapt the terminology. 
•	Countries adapt the software.
What I think we've accomplished today
Without writing a single page of the example Blueprint, we've clarified several foundational ideas:
1.	Blueprints are centred on business capabilities, not business processes. 
2.	Blueprints are outcome-oriented rather than terminology-oriented. 
3.	Profiles illustrate different operational contexts without changing the underlying Blueprint. 
4.	The Running Example (Sentara) is the vehicle for demonstrating those Profiles throughout the Blueprint.

## Design Journal Entry 004 – Capability Boundaries and Interfaces

This entry would capture:

Capabilities are defined by their outcome and the decision they enable.
Capabilities terminate at a clear business decision.
Downstream capabilities begin from that decision.
A capability may consume outputs from other capabilities without requiring those capabilities to be digitized.
Blueprints should support incremental digital transformation through modular, independently adoptable capabilities.

Capabilities are connected by business decisions
They are not connected by outputs -  The interface isn't the data.
It's the decision.

Capability Independence
Each Digitalization Blueprint shall describe a capability that can be implemented independently of the digital maturity of upstream or downstream capabilities.

I'm becoming convinced that Blueprints should almost never describe workflows.
Instead they should describe:
•	objectives, 
•	inputs, 
•	decisions, 
•	outputs, 
•	implementation elements. 
*The workflow belongs to the country*
This reinforces our capability-oriented philosophy.

## Design Journal Entry 007 – Baseline-Driven Digital Transformation

Observation

Veterinary Services almost always begin digital transformation with an existing operational capability rather than a blank slate.

Implications

Blueprints should support the evolution of existing practices.
The first Building Block should help countries understand their current implementation before designing future improvements.
Building Blocks should be framed as implementation concerns rather than prescribed solutions or workflow steps.

A *Building Block* is a *reusable implementation concern* that every Veterinary Service should consider when developing a digital capability. A Building Block is a reusable implementation concern that is explored through a structured design conversation and supported by reusable decision-support resources.

A *Blueprint* is a structured design conversation.
It guides a Veterinary Service through the implementation concerns that must be addressed to develop a digital capability, while allowing each country to determine the implementation most appropriate to its context..

A *Digitalization Blueprint* is not an implementation manual or software specification. It is a structured design guide that supports Veterinary Services in making informed implementation decisions while adapting the capability to their own operational, organizational and technological context.

A Blueprint facilitates informed decisions; the supporting resources provide the depth required to make those decisions confidently.

A Blueprint tells you what you need to decide, why it matters, what to consider, and where to find the detailed guidance.

--

## Foundational Design Principles". 

The Design System is a decision-support framework for digital transformation.
A Blueprint is the facilitator, not the solution.
A Blueprint is not an implementation manual or software specification; it is a structured design guide that supports informed implementation decisions.
Building Blocks represent implementation concerns, not workflow steps or software components.
Each Building Block is explored through a structured design conversation.
Every Building Block should ultimately be supported by decision-support resources (datasets, templates, standards, examples, reference models, interoperability guidance, etc.) and, where appropriate, implementation assets.
